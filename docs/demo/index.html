It tells you when it doesn't know

A gesture decoder usually names one gesture for every 50 ms of forearm muscle signal, right or wrong. This one answers with a set and a promise: the gesture you are holding is in the set 90% of the time. One gesture in the set means it's sure. Several mean it's torn, and a prosthesis or a game can wait instead of guessing.

The hard part is keeping that promise after the armband comes off and goes back on. Below is one person on four different days, months after the decoder was trained.

Try this

  1. Switch to the textbook recipe: the usual score, its threshold set on days 11-30, and no recalibration. It makes the same 90% promise and keeps it less and less as the days go by. Try day 91.
  2. Tick Recalibrate with the textbook recipe. A second of each gesture from today gets close on some days and not on others: its probabilities are nearly all 0 or 1, so the threshold has nothing to work with.
  3. Lift electrode 1 without the quality monitor. The sets start missing: a broken electrode doesn't make the decoder unsure, it makes it wrong. Turn the monitor on and the promise holds again, with bigger sets, because four electrodes became three.

These are the decoder's real outputs on MultiDay days 31, 61, 91 and 121 (one person, electrodes re-applied every day; trained on days 1-10). They were computed with the library by myobridge bench demo, and this page only plays them back and counts. The decoder is an LDA on 15 features per electrode; the sets come from ConformalGestures. Now break one yourself

This part runs the myobridge Python library itself in your browser (via Pyodide) on a different dataset, 3DC. It calibrates on part of a person's session, then streams the rest through the live pipeline 20 samples at a time, exactly as packets arrive from the armband. Pick a fault for any electrode while it plays.

Setup

This downloads Python, NumPy and SciPy (about 30 MB, cached afterwards) and about 7 MB of recordings, so it's best on Wi-Fi.

What you're seeing in the live part

  • The model is LDA on 15 features per electrode, trained on the person's first two cycles through the 11 gestures (about 2 minutes of data). It then decodes their first cycle of the second half of the session, which it never saw.
  • The set's threshold comes from those two training cycles (each scored by a model fitted on the other), with the temperature bench conformal chose on people 1-6.
  • An electrode coming off or clipping makes the decoder drop that channel and carry on with the others, using the same model minus that electrode's features and the same threshold. Mains hum and motion are flagged but kept, because the filters already remove most of them. With the monitor switch off, flagged electrodes stay in.
  • The trace shows the raw signal, before filtering, which is what the monitor looks at.

Recordings: MultiDay, Kaufmann, Englehart & Platzner, IEEE EMBC 2010, and 3DC, Côté-Allard et al., Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology 8:158 (2020), both as mirrored by the LibEMG project. This site holds no EMG: the first part ships only decoded sets, and for the live part your browser loads the recordings directly from LibEMG's repository.
